# SynchroFit resistance-training prescription policy

Updated: 2026-09-05  
Population: Healthy adult users performing general resistance training

## Recommended decision

Do not assign reps and rest from fitness level alone. Use:

- fitness level for starting volume and progression;
- training goal and exercise type for repetitions and rest;
- recovery and performance feedback for later adjustments.

Advanced users should not automatically receive more exercises or shorter rest. Their program should use deliberate exercise selection, bounded weekly volume, and limited training to failure.

## App-ready defaults

Working sets exclude warm-ups.

| Fitness level | Sets per exercise | Starting weekly sets per muscle | Target effort |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Beginner | 2 | 4-8 | Stop with 3-4 reps in reserve |
| Intermediate | 3 | 8-12 | Stop with 2-3 reps in reserve |
| Advanced | 3; up to 4 on one priority lift | 10-16 | Stop with 1-3 reps in reserve |

| Goal and exercise type | Rep target | Rest after each set |
|---|---:|---:|
| Strength - primary compound | 3-6 | 180 seconds; advanced users may use up to 300 seconds |
| Hypertrophy - compound | 6-12 | 120-180 seconds |
| Hypertrophy - isolation or machine | 10-20 | 60-120 seconds |
| Muscular endurance | 12-20 | 60-90 seconds |
| Timed bodyweight or isometric | 20-45 seconds | 60-120 seconds |

Fallback when exercise type is unknown: **2/3/3 sets by level, 8-12 reps, and 120 seconds rest**. Do not shorten rest merely because experience increases.

## Anti-redundancy and fatigue safeguards

- Tag exercises by movement pattern, primary and secondary muscles, equipment, and angle/region.
- Allow no more than two exercises in a session with the same primary muscle and movement pattern; the second must add a distinct angle, region, or resistance profile.
- Prefer 4-6 resistance exercises per workout and cap one muscle at 10 direct sets per session.
- Count direct sets as 1.0 and compound secondary-muscle sets as 0.5 toward weekly volume.
- Retain core exercises for 4-8 weeks. Replace them deliberately, not randomly.
- Do not routinely prescribe compound lifts to failure. Failure, if allowed, should be limited to the final set of a low-risk isolation or machine exercise.
- Hold or reduce volume when reps fall more than 20% across sets, performance declines over two sessions, or soreness/joint discomfort persists.
- Increase volume for one priority muscle at a time; 12-20 weekly sets is an advanced hypertrophy option, not a universal default.

## Why this policy

The [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/) found that strength responds especially to heavier loads and 2-3 sets, hypertrophy responds to higher weekly volume, and momentary failure or complex methods do not consistently improve results. A large [network meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/) similarly found higher loads ranked best for strength while multiset work was important for hypertrophy.

Rest should preserve set quality. A [2024 rest-interval meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/) found a small hypertrophy advantage above 60 seconds, while the [IUSCA position stand](https://journal.iusca.org/index.php/Journal/article/download/81/140/) advises at least two minutes for multi-joint exercises and 60-90 seconds for single-joint or suitable machine exercises.

Failure is a poor default fatigue strategy: a [hypertrophy meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36334240/) found no clear advantage for momentary failure, and a [controlled fatigue study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36752989/) found markedly more fatigue and worse perceptual responses at failure than with reps in reserve.

For trained users, [12-20 weekly sets per muscle](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35291645/) is a reasonable optional hypertrophy range, but greater volume shows diminishing returns and more than 20 sets did not consistently improve all muscles. Finally, an [exercise-variation systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35438660/) concluded that systematic variation can help, while excessive random variation and redundant stimuli may hinder adaptation.

## Safety boundary

These are starting rules for healthy adults, not medical or rehabilitation prescriptions. Age, injury, health conditions, sleep, diet, and concurrent sport affect recovery. The app should offer conservative defaults, monitor response, and recommend professional guidance when pain, injury, or medical limitations are present.
